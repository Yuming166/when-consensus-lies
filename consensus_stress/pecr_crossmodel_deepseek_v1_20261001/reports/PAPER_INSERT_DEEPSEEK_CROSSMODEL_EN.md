## Suggested paper insert

To test whether the bidirectional response signal is specific to one model family, we repeated the frozen three-world collection on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. Prompts, item order, temperature, expected directions, parser, scoring rules, and the primary comparison were frozen before calls; the only model-specific change was a uniform low-reasoning-effort setting required to avoid truncation. The provider exhausted API credit during collection. Under the no-retry protocol, the eligible strict subset retained 1,192/2,225 attempted items (53.6%; 743 error and 449 correct originals) from 395 source groups. On this quota-truncated subset, three-world Curve HGB reached an error-AUROC of 0.8153 versus 0.8015 for the same-budget ordinary three-world HGB, a paired difference of +0.0138 (source-group bootstrap 95% CI [0.0016, 0.0255]). This positive interval replicates the Qwen development contrast (+0.0160; 95% CI [0.0041, 0.0269]).

We therefore describe this as cross-model robustness evidence on a non-random available subset, not independent confirmation: API failures after credit exhaustion caused 46.4% item loss, and no missing slot was retried. In secondary descriptive transfers, a DeepSeek-trained Curve model reached 0.8075 AUROC on Qwen, whereas a Qwen-trained model reached 0.7418 on DeepSeek. The paper consequently claims replication of the paired curve gain within DeepSeek, not symmetric transfer or holdout generalization.
